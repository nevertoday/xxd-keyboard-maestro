If you teach, it has probably happened to you once. You plug your laptop into the projector, open the lid, and last night's music blasts through the classroom while email alerts pop up one after another on the big screen. There are only two things you need to do when you walk into class, turn the sound off and quit Mail, but on a busy day you forget. When you finish this page, your MacBook will mute itself and quit Mail as soon as it joins the school Wi-Fi, without you pressing anything.

Time triggers handle "what time is it." The two <Term k="触发器" text="triggers" /> on this page handle "where are you, and what's plugged in." <mark>For the things you always do when you arrive somewhere or plug something in, let that network or that device press start.</mark>

## Join the school Wi-Fi, and it mutes and quits Mail

Make a new <Term k="宏" /> called "Into class." For the trigger, choose **Wireless Network**, which runs when a Wi-Fi network is connected to or disconnected from ([Wireless Network trigger](https://wiki.keyboardmaestro.com/trigger/Wireless_Network)). You can have it respond to any network, or only to a matching name, and the wiki prefers the BSSID, a more unique ID, since lots of people have networks named "Home" or "Work."

You don't have to type the name. Put your cursor in the name field of the trigger, then disconnect and rejoin the school Wi-Fi from the menu bar, and Keyboard Maestro fills in the network's name (or BSSID) for you. Have it run on connecting.

Add two actions. **Mute Audio Device** can mute, unmute or toggle mute on the default audio device (Keyboard Maestro 11 and later, [Mute Audio Device](https://wiki.keyboardmaestro.com/action/Mute_Audio_Device)), so choose mute. Then add **Quit a Specific Application**, pick Mail, and leave the option on **Quit**. If you want things back after class, make a second macro, "Out of class," triggered when the same network disconnects, with an action that unmutes.

From macOS Sonoma on, there's one step you can't skip: reading Wi-Fi information needs the Location permission. The wiki says to make sure Keyboard Maestro Engine has it, which you'll find in **System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security**, under Location Services. Without it, the Engine can't read the Wi-Fi information.

<Callout type="ok">
In the classroom, turn Wi-Fi off and on again. Once it rejoins the school network, the sound icon shows muted and Mail has quit.
</Callout>

## It will run more than once, so make it safe to

The wiki makes a point of this. Your Mac disconnects and reconnects Wi-Fi every time it sleeps and wakes, and macOS sometimes reconnects quietly in the background too, so these macros should be safe to run several times. Muting and quitting Mail are fine. Muting something already muted, or quitting an app that's already closed, breaks nothing. But if you put "create today's lesson notes" in there, you'd get an extra file every time you closed the lid. Pick actions that end up the same no matter how often they run.

## Turn on the scanner, and the scanning app opens

Now a different reader. If you have kids in school, you probably scan a few forms some evenings, and every time it's turn on the scanner, hunt down the scanning app, and forget to close it afterwards. The **USB Device** trigger runs a macro when a USB device is attached or detached, and the wiki's own example is exactly this: launch your scanner software when you turn on the scanner, and quit it when you turn it off ([USB Device trigger](https://wiki.keyboardmaestro.com/trigger/USB_Device)).

USB device names often aren't what you'd expect, so let it fill this one in too. Put your cursor in the trigger's name field, then plug in (or turn on) the scanner, and the name appears. Two macros keep it clear. One runs when the device is attached, with **Activate a Specific Application** set to your scanning app, which launches it if it isn't running. The other runs when the device is detached, with **Quit a Specific Application** for the same app. While the macro runs, the `%TriggerValue%` <Term k="令牌" /> holds the device's name, which is handy if you want a notification to say which device it was.

If what you're waiting for isn't a device but a disk, like an external drive or a memory card in a card reader, the trigger you want is **Mounted Volume**. It runs when a volume is mounted or unmounted, either a specific one or any volume ([Mounted Volume trigger](https://wiki.keyboardmaestro.com/trigger/Mounted_Volume)).

## Just checking from inside another macro

Sometimes you don't need a macro to start on connecting. You only want another macro to adapt, say a morning reminder that shows at home but not at school. Conditions do that: the **Wireless Network** condition checks whether a network is connected, and the **USB Device** condition checks whether a device is there, used with **If Then Else** ([Wireless Network condition](https://wiki.keyboardmaestro.com/condition/Wireless_Network), [USB Device condition](https://wiki.keyboardmaestro.com/condition/USB_Device)). For what conditions are and how they work, see [full guide step 4](/en/guide/4-variables/).

## Stuck?

<Q q="I joined the network and the macro didn't run.">
On macOS Sonoma and later, first check that Keyboard Maestro Engine has the Location permission. Then check that the name in the trigger matches the network exactly. The surest way is to clear it, put the cursor in, and disconnect and reconnect so it fills itself in. Also make sure the macro and its <Term k="宏组" /> are both enabled.
</Q>
<Q q="The macro ran several times in one day.">
That's expected. Sleeping, waking and the system's own reconnects all run it again. Switch to actions that don't mind repeating, and keep anything that creates files or sends messages out of this kind of macro.
</Q>
<Q q="The scanner's name isn't what I thought, and typing it in doesn't work.">
Don't type the name. Let Keyboard Maestro fill it in when you attach the device. The wiki says plugging in or turning on both work, so for a scanner with a power button, put the cursor in place first and then switch it on.
</Q>
<Q q="I plugged in an external drive and the USB Device trigger did nothing.">
For waiting on a disk, the **Mounted Volume** trigger is the better fit, since it goes by the volume's name.
</Q>

One network or one device is enough to press start for you. To have a macro start on the hour as well, see [Run something at the same time every day](/en/howto/daily-schedule/).
