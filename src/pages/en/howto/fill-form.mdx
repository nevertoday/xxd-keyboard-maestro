Freelancers who find work through one project site often bid on several jobs a day there, and every bid form asks the same fields from scratch: name, email, phone, portfolio link. Four fields typed twenty times, and it's easy to get one digit of your phone number wrong, which means the client can't reach you. With this macro, you'll click into the first field and press ⌃⌥⌘F (⌃ is Control, ⌥ is Option, ⌘ is Command), and all four fields fill in at once. The cursor stops in the last field, you check it over, and you click submit yourself.

<mark>In a form, Tab is what moves you from one field to the next, and a macro does the same: type a field, press Tab, type the next one.</mark>

## Walk through it yourself first

Before you build anything, open the form, click into the first field, and just press Tab, one press at a time, watching where the cursor goes. Most forms go top to bottom, but some sites slip a pop-up menu or an "I agree" checkbox in between, and those take a Tab too. Note the order down. The macro's whole job is to replay it.

That also tells you something: one macro fits one form. A different site will probably order its fields differently, so make another macro for it.

## Build the macro

First make a macro group called "Forms", and in its settings choose **Available in these applications:** and add Safari, or whichever browser you use. That way ⌃⌥⌘F only works in your browser and stays free everywhere else.

In that group, make a new macro. For the trigger, choose **Hot Key** and press ⌃⌥⌘F. There are two actions.

The first is **Insert Text by Pasting**, with your name in it. Why give the name an action of its own? Because the other action, **Insert Text by Typing**, presses keys for you one at a time, and the official page says it can only type characters you can type on the keyboard with one or two keystrokes (see [Insert Text by Typing](https://wiki.keyboardmaestro.com/action/Insert_Text_by_Typing)). Plenty of names have characters outside that, Chinese characters for example, and pasting handles any of them.

<Copy id="ff-name" title="First action: your name" code="Sam Lee" />

The second action is **Insert Text by Typing**, with the other fields written together and a `%Tab%` between each. `%Tab%` is a <Term k="令牌" />: when the macro runs, it becomes a real Tab press, which moves to the next field. That's exactly what the official page recommends: when you want Tab or Return to move between fields, use typing. The `%Tab%` at the start moves the cursor out of the name field first:

<Copy id="ff-rest" title="Second action: the other fields" code="%Tab%sam.lee@example.com%Tab%000 1234 5678%Tab%https://example.com/sam" />

Swap the examples for your own email, phone and link. The second action only types and leaves the clipboard alone, so following the paste with it doesn't cause the wrong-paste problem.

<Callout type="warn">Don't put Return at the end, and don't have the macro click submit. Let the filled form sit there so you can read it over, while there's still time to fix anything. Keep passwords out of the macro too. The official [Typed String trigger](https://wiki.keyboardmaestro.com/trigger/Typed_String) page calls typing into password fields this way a serious security concern.</Callout>

## Check that it works

Open the site's bid form, click into the name field, and press ⌃⌥⌘F. You should see your name appear, then the cursor jump down field by field as the email, phone and link go in, stopping in the link field. Read each one. If a field landed in the wrong place, go back to the walk-through and see where the Tab order differs from what you expected.

## Stuck?

<Q q="The text went in, but some letters are missing or out of order.">
Web form fields often check what you type as you type it, and when Keyboard Maestro types too fast, characters get dropped or jumbled. The official fix is to add a **Set Action Delay** action at the start of this macro to slow its typing down. Use it for this macro only, not as a global setting. See [the Troubleshooting section on missing or jumbled characters](https://wiki.keyboardmaestro.com/Troubleshooting#Sometimes_Typing_results_in_Characters_Missing_or_out_of_Order).
</Q>
<Q q="The name field got whatever I copied last.">
The clipboard really does hold your name by then. It's the app that kept its own copy of the old clipboard and didn't notice the change. The official [Sometimes the Wrong Thing is Pasted](https://wiki.keyboardmaestro.com/Troubleshooting#Sometimes_the_Wrong_Thing_is_Pasted) section says this is most common in cross-platform apps. If you run into it, the easy way out is to type your name yourself and let the macro start from the second field: delete the first action, and the `%Tab%` at the start of the second.
</Q>
<Q q="Everything shifts by one field partway through.">
There's probably a pop-up menu, checkbox or button you didn't notice before that field, and it took a Tab. Add or remove one `%Tab%` there.
</Q>
<Q q="The email field filled up with input-method candidates instead of letters.">
**Insert Text by Typing** presses one key at a time, and the official page says it's just like you typing, so if a Chinese or similar input method is on, it takes the letters of your email as its own input. Switch to a plain keyboard before you press ⌃⌥⌘F. The name is pasted, so the input method doesn't touch it.
</Q>
<Q q="I pressed ⌃⌥⌘F and nothing happened.">
Make sure your browser is in front. The macro group is set to work only there, so pressing it in any other app does nothing. If the browser is in front and still nothing, look in the editor to see that "Forms" and the macro are still ticked and that the Engine is running. If it still won't fire, see [full guide step 6](/en/guide/6-debug/).
</Q>

Build one, and every bid after that is twenty-odd fewer things to type and one less chance of a typo. For the field you fill in fresh each time, like which project you're bidding on, the macro can ask you first and then fill it in. See [Ask a question before doing the work](/en/howto/ask-input/).
